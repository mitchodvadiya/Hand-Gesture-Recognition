Abstract—Predicting hand gestures from surface electromyography (sEMG) is challenging because bio-potential signals are inherently noisy, subject-dependent, and non-stationary. In continuous acquisition streams, two physiological and computational bottlenecks arise: spatial crosstalk across adjacent forearm muscles that degrades discrimination between anatomical antagonists, and severe frame-level class imbalance (~10:1 ratio) caused by predominant resting frames (noGesture), which induces severe gesture under-detection under standard Cross-Entropy loss. This paper implements an improved spatial-temporal gesture classification framework following the benchmark Inception CNN-LSTM model of Barona López et al. [1] on the public EMG-EPN-612 dataset, and compares four architectures on a strictly disjoint 5-participant cohort (750 training vs. 750 validation continuous sequences): a Base Inception CNN, the reference Base Inception CNN-LSTM, an Enhanced CNN with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) channel attention, and a proposed AttnInceptionHGR architecture fusing SE channel attention, an Inception backbone, a 256-dimensional bottleneck projection, a Bidirectional LSTM, and 4-head Temporal Self-Attention under Class-Weighted Focal Loss (γ = 2.0). On the held-out 750-sequence validation set, the proposed model reached 72.00% sequence accuracy, 72.60% Macro F1, 77.32% Macro Precision and 72.00% Macro Recall, compared to 70.67%, 68.76%, 74.89% and 70.67% for the reference CNN-LSTM baseline (+3.84 percentage points gain in Macro F1). Crucially, the proposed architecture reduced active gesture sequences incorrectly classified as resting state by 78.26% (from 92 to 20 missed gesture trials), raising resting-class precision from 57.60% to 85.92% and F1-score from 73.10% to 91.39%. Offline CPU inference latency was 53.84 ms per 24-window sequence against 49.17 ms for the baseline. We interpret the differences, trade-offs in individual gestures, and discuss limitations: a single training run, a 5-subject validation cohort, and offline batch evaluation.

Keywords—surface electromyography, hand gesture recognition, Inception network, bidirectional LSTM, channel attention, focal loss, time-series classification, myoelectric control.

I. INTRODUCTION

Forecasting discrete gesture intent from continuous forearm surface electromyography (sEMG) is a central problem in active myoelectric prosthetics, neuro-rehabilitation, and human-computer interaction (HCI) [1], [2]. Non-invasive surface electrodes detect compound motor unit action potentials (MUAP) generated during voluntary contractions. However, the signal-to-noise ratio is low, muscle fatigue induces non-stationary spectral drift, and cross-user anatomical variability causes models trained on one participant to degrade on unseen individuals [3], [4].

In continuous naturalistic interaction, two fundamental signal-processing challenges persist:

1. Electrode Spatial Crosstalk: Compact consumer arrays (such as the 8-channel Myo Armband) record composite potentials from overlapping muscular compartments. Antagonistic gestures sharing common muscular substrates (such as open hand extension and waveOut wrist abduction) produce correlated spatial activations across adjacent channels.

2. Acute Resting Frame Imbalance: Continuous interaction comprises brief voluntary muscle contractions (1.0–2.0 s) interspersed with prolonged resting intervals. Sliding-window segmentation across continuous trials produces an acute frame distribution wherein resting state (noGesture) accounts for over 68.4% of all sliding frames. Under standard Cross-Entropy loss, classifiers suffer from gesture under-detection, erroneously predicting active muscle contractions as resting state.

Barona López et al. [1] proposed combining an Inception Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with an unrolled Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network and a modal majority post-processor on the public EMG-EPN-612 dataset, reporting 90.55% ± 9.45% recognition accuracy across 306 test subjects. This is the base paper of the present study. Two key questions follow from it. First, can integrating Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) channel attention dynamically recalibrate electrode channels and resolve spatial crosstalk between antagonistic muscle groups? Second, does introducing a 256-dimensional bottleneck projection resolve the 53× parameter bloat of the reference paper's uncompressed LSTM (22,464 input features) while enabling bidirectional temporal modeling and Class-Weighted Focal Loss to mitigate resting-state gesture under-detection in low-data regimes?

The contributions of this paper are:

• (a) a leakage-controlled, participant-independent pipeline in which 5 training participants (750 sequences) and 5 validation participants (750 sequences) have strictly zero overlap (Train ∩ Val = ∅) with per-trial normalization;

• (b) an implementation and empirical evaluation of four distinct sEMG architectures (Base Inception CNN, Base Inception CNN-LSTM, Enhanced Inception CNN, and Proposed AttnInceptionHGR) under identical training and evaluation settings;

• (c) an 88.6% compression of the recurrent input dimension (22,464 → 256), halving parameter overhead while introducing bidirectional recurrence and multi-head temporal self-attention;

• (d) a technical discussion of gesture-specific trade-offs, showing that Class-Weighted Focal Loss reduces missed active gestures misclassified as resting state by 78.26% (from 92 to 20 missed trials), along with an explicit statement of limitations regarding cohort size and offline inference.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows: Section II describes the methodology, Section III presents and discusses the experimental results, and Section IV concludes.

II. METHODOLOGY

A. Overall Research Methodology

The workflow comprises seven sequential steps: (1) obtain the public EMG-EPN-612 benchmark dataset; (2) clean, normalize, rectify, and filter the raw 8-channel sEMG signals; (3) segment continuous trials into 24 overlapping sliding frames of 1.5 s each; (4) extract 24-point STFT spectrograms across 13 discrete frequency bins (0–12 Hz); (5) split samples into strictly disjoint training and validation cohorts by participant ID; (6) train four model architectures using validation-based early stopping; and (7) evaluate sequence-level performance with Mode 1-1 modal post-processing.

D. Data Pre-processing and Feature Extraction

Cleaning and Normalization: Raw 8-channel signals are inspected for transmission dropouts. If max |x| > 1.0, signals are divided by 128.0 (half-scale of 8-bit ADC). Full-wave absolute rectification |x[n]| is calculated across all channels to obtain muscular energy envelopes.

Butterworth Low-Pass Filtering: A 5th-order digital Butterworth filter with normalized cutoff Wn = 0.10 (fc = 10 Hz) is applied along the time axis using zero-phase forward-backward filtering (scipy.signal.filtfilt), exactly reproducing the filter order and cutoff frequency of [1].

Sliding Windowing: Continuous 5.0-second trials (1,000 samples) are partitioned into sliding frames of length L = 300 samples (1.5 s) with step size ΔL = 30 samples (0.15 s stride), producing T = 24 overlapping frames per trial.

STFT Spectrogram Representation: For each 300-sample frame, a 24-point Short-Time Fourier Transform with 50% overlap (step = 12) is evaluated across 13 frequency bins (0 to 12 Hz) using a periodic Hanning window:

w[n] = 0.5 · (1 − cos(2πn / N)),    n ∈ {0, ..., 23}    (1)
Wk,n = exp(−j · 2π fk n / fs),    fk ∈ {0, ..., 12} Hz    (2)

Each frame yields a tensor Xt ∈ ℜ8 × 13 × 24 (Channels × Frequencies × Time steps), forming continuous sequence tensors S ∈ ℜ24 × 8 × 13 × 24.

Participant-Independent Partition: The 10 participants are partitioned into strictly disjoint subsets (Train ∩ Val = ∅). No data from validation participants is observed during training, feature scaling, or class-weight calculation.

E. Algorithm Description

1) Algorithm 1 – Base Inception CNN

Reproduces the spatial feature extractor of Barona López et al. [1]. The 6-module Inception backbone processes each frame independently. Features are flattened (72 × 13 × 24 = 22,464) and mapped directly to 6 classes via a linear layer without temporal recurrence. Trained using standard Cross-Entropy loss.

2) Algorithm 2 – Base Inception CNN-LSTM (Base Paper Reproduction)

Exact PyTorch re-implementation of the base paper architecture [1]. The 6-module Inception backbone extracts spatial representations from each frame. The raw output (22,464 dimensions) is unrolled into a unidirectional LSTM with 128 hidden units, followed by a linear classification head (128 → 6) and standard Cross-Entropy loss. This model incurs 11.65 million learnable parameters (53× the parameter count of the CNN).

3) Algorithm 3 – Enhanced Inception CNN

Augments Algorithm 1 with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) channel attention across the 8 electrode channels, a dense projection head (Linear 22,464 → 256, LayerNorm, ReLU, Dropout 0.30), and Class-Weighted Focal Loss.

4) Algorithm 4 – Proposed AttnInceptionHGR

Our primary proposed architecture comprises six integrated stages:

1. SE Channel Attention: Squeezes each frame X ∈ ℜ8 × 13 × 24 via spatial average pooling zc = (1 / (FW)) ∑i=113 ∑j=124 Xc,i,j, and gates electrode channels via:

s = σ(W2 · ReLU(W1 z))      (3)

where W1 ∈ ℜ4 × 8, W2 ∈ ℜ8 × 4, and σ(·) is sigmoid. Calibrated input: X̃ = s ⊙ X.

2. Inception Backbone: 6 multi-scale blocks (1×1, 3×3, 5×5, maxpool) with residual additions joining modules 1a–1c and 1c–1e, outputting 72 feature maps and 2D Batch Normalization.

3. Bottleneck Dimensionality Projection: Compresses 22,464 flattened features to 256 dimensions:

ht = Dropout(ReLU(LayerNorm(Wp ft + bp)), p=0.30)    (4)

where Wp ∈ ℜ256 × 22,464, reducing recurrent input dimensions by 88.6%.

4. Bidirectional LSTM: A 2-layer BiLSTM (hidden = 64 per direction, total 128) captures past and future temporal context across the 24 time steps:

Ht = [→ht ∥ ←ht] ∈ ℜ128      (5)

5. Temporal Attention: A 4-head self-attention module attends to contraction bursts:

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q KT / √dk) V    (6)
Z = LayerNorm(H + MHA(H, H, H))    (7)

6. Class-Weighted Focal Loss: Formulated as:

ℒFocal = −∑c=16 αc (1 − pt,c)γ log(pt,c)    (8)

with focusing parameter γ = 2.0, label smoothing ε = 0.05, and class weighting αc computed dynamically from training batch frequencies.

B. Quantitative Analysis

Comparing Algorithm 1 (Base CNN) and Algorithm 2 (Base CNN-LSTM) confirms the observations of Barona López et al. [1]: adding recurrent cells improves post-processed sequence accuracy from 62.40% to 70.67% (+8.27 percentage points) and Macro F1 from 55.54% to 68.76% (+13.22 percentage points). In our trial-level setting, temporal recurrence captures continuous contraction dynamics that frame-independent CNNs miss.

Algorithm 3 (Enhanced CNN with Focal Loss) achieved only 42.80% accuracy, demonstrating that without recurrent temporal context, frame-independent focal loss leads to severe gradient instability during transient gesture onsets.

Algorithm 4 (Proposed AttnInceptionHGR) reached the highest overall sequence accuracy (72.00%) and highest Macro F1-score (72.60%), improving over the reference base paper by +1.33% in accuracy (10 additional correct sequences out of 750) and +3.84 percentage points in Macro F1.

D. Discussion of Results

Reduction of Active Gestures Misclassified as Resting State: In baseline CNN-LSTM, 92 active gesture sequences (20 fist, 13 open, 49 pinch, 9 waveIn, 1 waveOut) were erroneously misclassified as noGesture, caused by Cross-Entropy loss succumbing to 68.4% resting frame dominance (precision = 57.60%). Our proposed model reduced these active gesture sequences misclassified as noGesture to 20 (a 78.26% reduction in missed gestures), raising resting-class precision to 85.92% and F1 to 91.39% (+18.29 percentage points). Concurrently, 3 actual resting trials were misclassified as pinch (slight false activation during rest, yielding 97.60% recall vs. 100.0% in baseline).

Resolution of Crosstalk vs. Class-Specific Trade-offs: For waveOut, proposed model achieved 100.0% precision (0 false positives), completely preventing erroneous wrist extension commands during other actions. However, waveOut recall dropped from 97.60% to 69.60% due to 34 trials being misclassified as open, reflecting remaining shared extensor activation. For pinch, recall doubled from 34.40% to 68.80% (F1: 47.25% to 51.65%), but precision was constrained to 41.35% due to false positives from fist (50 trials) and open (58 trials) due to partial finger flexion overlap.

Inference Latency and Real-Time Considerations: On a quad-core CPU, average offline inference latency was 53.84 ms per continuous sequence, comfortably below the 100–125 ms controller delay threshold cited in prosthetic literature [14], [15].

Limitations: (1) The evaluation uses 5 training subjects and 5 validation subjects; absolute accuracies cannot be compared directly with the 90.55% reported on 306 subjects in [1]. (2) Single-run execution without confidence intervals. (3) Offline non-causal processing (bilateral context and whole-trial filtering).

IV. CONCLUSION

This paper implemented and compared four sEMG gesture recognition architectures based on the benchmark of Barona López et al. [1]. By introducing Squeeze-and-Excitation channel attention, a 256-dimensional bottleneck projection, a Bidirectional LSTM, and Class-Weighted Focal Loss, the proposed AttnInceptionHGR architecture achieved 72.00% sequence accuracy and 72.60% Macro F1 on the EMG-EPN-612 validation cohort, outperforming our re-implementation of the baseline CNN-LSTM (+3.84 percentage points Macro F1) and cutting missed active gestures misclassified as resting state by 78.26%. Future work will expand training across the full 306-subject cohort, report per-window recognition accuracy, and develop causal online models for embedded edge microcontrollers.
